Reviewed 2026-11-15. Dataset: **38 exercises**, including **5 exercises with triceps as the primary muscle**. All 10 `MuscleId` values occur as primary muscles; all 8 `EquipmentId` values and all 14 `MovementId` values are represented.

## Primary source

The [American Council on Exercise (ACE) Exercise Library](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/) is the direct publisher of the exercise descriptions used here. Its individual pages were opened and their main exercise descriptions inspected with Aside Browser. The table links each dataset ID to the exact page used. Korean names, concise instructions, and tips are original paraphrases of those descriptions rather than copied translations. No source images, video, or media are redistributed.

## Data conventions and limitations

- Primary muscles describe the main force producers; secondary muscles describe selected assisting or stabilizing groups. These are editorial anatomical mappings to the contract's 10 broad categories, not EMG rankings or exhaustive anatomy. For example, `back` combines the latissimus, scapular muscles, and spinal extensors; `abs` includes trunk stabilization. Forearm muscles, brachialis, hip adductors, and other groups outside the enum cannot be represented. A hammer curl also trains the brachialis and brachioradialis, beyond the available `biceps` label. Empty secondary arrays are deliberate.
- The exercise descriptions were checked individually; ACE category/filter pages sometimes list surprisingly broad body parts, so those broad labels were not copied as primary-muscle assignments. No percentage activation or medical benefit is claimed.
- Every item in an exercise's equipment array is required. Bodyweight is an always-available capability and is listed for exercises needing no apparatus; it is not redundantly added to weighted exercises. A dumbbell bench press requires **both** `dumbbell` and `bench`, so availability matching must use `every()`. As specified in CONTRACT.md, selecting no equipment should show all exercises until the user enables the equipment-only toggle.
- `bench` includes the relevant flat or adjustable bench: incline press/reverse fly and seated supported curls/presses require an adjustable backrest. `machine` means the particular dedicated machine named by the exercise, including its built-in seat, pads, handles and foot supports; owning an arbitrary machine does not imply all machine exercises are possible. `cable` includes a compatible adjustable pulley and the rope attachment used in the pressdown. The fixed enum cannot distinguish attachments, machine models, or bench angles. No rack-dependent squat or barbell bench press is included because a rack is not an available enum.
- The calf entry follows ACE's **seated, straight-leg calf press** on a footplate machine, not a seated bent-knee soleus raise or a standing shoulder-pad machine. The elevated bridge follows the dumbbell-loaded upper-back-on-bench version. The Bulgarian split squat uses one dumbbell held at the chest. The rope pressdown follows source 3 specifically.
- `movement` is an animation family, not a complete biomechanics taxonomy. Chin-ups and pull-ups share `pulldown`; a leg curl shares `curl`; bridges and Romanian deadlifts share `hinge`; the rear-foot-elevated split squat shares `lunge`. Exact setup comes from the exercise instructions.
- Difficulty is a coarse app label based on coordination, support, and loading demands; it is not an individualized assessment or a guarantee that an exercise suits everyone. Source pages do not establish a universal ranking across exercises.
- **3 sets** and the default repetition counts are editable product starting values, not source-prescribed programs. Unilateral repetition counts are **per side**; both sides complete a set. The front plank uses **1 repetition = one 20-second hold**, explicitly stated in its instructions/tip, because the contract offers only numeric sets/reps and no duration field. A later UI duration field would represent this more naturally.
- Instructions compress setup and execution into four steps and omit the sources' optional progressions and some spotting detail. They are educational summaries, not medical advice, rehabilitation instructions, or a personalized training prescription. Range-of-motion cues use controlled, comfortable movement rather than requiring a single depth for everyone.
- The library contains video-only/empty text pages as well: ACE kneeling lat pulldown (35) had no readable main instructions during inspection and was **excluded** rather than represented as verified instruction content. The final 38 entries each have readable source descriptions. Links and published content can change after the review date.

## Validation

- Imported the TypeScript module directly with Node 24's native type stripping and checked exact required object keys, value types/enums, positive integer defaults, nonempty Korean/English names, and Korean instructions/tips.
- Checked 38 unique, stable kebab-case IDs; no duplicate entries within muscle/equipment arrays; primary and secondary muscle arrays do not overlap.
- Every exercise has four Korean instruction steps and two Korean form tips. All exercises declare at least one primary muscle and at least one equipment value.
- Asserted coverage: 10/10 muscles as primary, 8/8 equipment values, 14/14 movement values, and 5 triceps-primary exercises.
- Ran the standalone TypeScript strict compiler check without writing build output or changing package files. Source-to-dataset mapping has exactly 38 rows, one per exercise.
